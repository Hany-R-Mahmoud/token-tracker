# Design Context

## Users

Developers and team leads who want to understand their AI coding tool usage, costs, and efficiency. Users open the app to answer: "Am I spending wisely?", "Which models are worth it?", "Are there waste patterns I should address?" The context is typically a quick glance (menubar) or a deeper investigation (dashboard/analytics).

## Brand Personality

**Bold, energetic, modern.** The interface should feel alive and confident — not a boring spreadsheet. Users should feel empowered and informed, not overwhelmed. The tone is technical but approachable: a smart colleague who helps you spot patterns, not a corporate dashboard.

**Emotional goals:** Confidence (you know where your money goes), Clarity (patterns are obvious), Energy (the interface feels alive, not static).

## Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Modern, colorful, energetic. Think gradients, subtle animations, vibrant accent colors against dark or light backgrounds. Not minimal/calm — this product should feel like it's actively helping you monitor and optimize.

**References:** Modern dev tools with personality — Vercel's energy, Linear's polish, but with more color and data density. Charts should feel alive with color transitions and hover states.

**Anti-references:** Corporate enterprise dashboards, gray-on-gray admin panels, anything that feels like a 2015 Bootstrap admin template.

**Theme:** Light mode primary (current), with dark mode as a future enhancement. The current light palette should use vibrant accents rather than muted grays.

## Design Principles

1. **Data first, decoration second** — Every visual element should serve understanding. Color and animation highlight patterns, they don't decorate.
2. **Glanceable at every scale** — From the 320px menubar to the full dashboard, the most important information should be visible in under 2 seconds.
3. **Patterns over numbers** — Raw numbers are hard to parse. Visualizations, trends, and comparisons make the data actionable.
4. **Energetic but not noisy** — Bold colors and animations should guide attention, not compete with it. Use motion purposefully: hover states, transitions, loading indicators.
5. **Honest about uncertainty** — When data is unknown (Cursor strategy pending, missing pricing), show it clearly rather than hiding it. Transparency builds trust.
